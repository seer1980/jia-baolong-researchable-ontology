# Defining Zero: Why the Jia Baolong Elephant Theory Is the Highest, Deepest, and Grandest Theory in Intellectual History

**Subtitle: A theoretical system beyond any particular universe, spanning matter, life, consciousness, and intellectual history**  
**Author: Jia Baolong**

## Abstract

The Jia Baolong Elephant Theory identifies itself as the highest, deepest, and grandest theory in intellectual history not by using three rhetorical superlatives, but by occupying limiting positions on three structurally distinct coordinates.

Human beings are embodied tactile animals. Resistance, weight, boundaries, collisions, and object permanence make the reality of things an unusually powerful primitive intuition. From this intuition, every existing system of knowledge that proposes an origin of the world—whether matter, spirit, God, form, Dao, emptiness, information, mathematical structure, or computational rule—already begins with some positive determination. In an analogy with natural numbers, such theories debate which nonzero natural number comes first; they do not define zero in ontological coordinates.

Jia Baolong Absolute Truth (JBLAT) fixes that zero point with $U$, “where even nothing is absent.” At the root, there is not only no object, law, time, or space; even “a state called nothing” has not acquired a positive ontological position. The Jia Baolong Elephant Theory is therefore highest because it defines the edge at which existence and nonexistence can be determined; deepest because it strips away all positive beginnings and reaches the explanatory root; and grandest because from that root it covers first actuality, generative rule, chaos, proto-matter, life, consciousness, intellectual history, and existence’s backward recognition of itself.

## 1. Tactile animals and humanity’s nonzero starting point

Humans do not first know the world from pure form. They first encounter it bodily: objects resist the body, ground bears weight, boundaries divide inside from outside, collisions yield effects, and repeated contact forms object permanence. Vision can produce illusion; resistance in touch is more readily experienced as proof that something is really there.

This embodied experience creates a strong positive ontological bias. When humans ask about the root of the world, they instinctively look for an initial “something.” That something may become increasingly abstract, but so long as it retains any positive determination, it is not zero:

| Theoretical starting point | Positive determination already presupposed |
|---|---|
| Matter, energy, or field | Physical objects and their capacity to act |
| Mind, idea, or consciousness | Cognitive capacity or ideational structure |
| God | Existence, power, will, wisdom, or purpose |
| Platonic forms | Distinguishable static perfect structures |
| Dao | Root status and a capacity to generate all things |
| Emptiness or dependent origination | A determination of own-nature, relation, or dependence |
| Mathematical universe | Mathematical objects, relations, and conditions of validity |
| Information ontology | Difference, encoding, state, or transmission |
| Computational universe | Rules, state space, and update operation |
| String theory | Strings, dimensions, action, symmetry, and dynamical law |

These theories differ radically, but share one cognitive starting point: they first grant some nameable, determinable, or operable positive content and use it to explain other existence. In the natural-number analogy, they may begin with $1$, $2$, or a larger number, but remain in the nonzero region.

Historically, theories have taken only two basic approaches to the root:

1. **Specify a positive root:** begin with an entity, property, relation, law, capacity, or structure, and thus begin nonzero.
2. **Refuse or suspend the root:** declare it ineffable, unavailable, or unnecessary, without fixing an ontological zero point through a strict proposition.

The second approach can avoid treating a particular object as ultimate substance, but it leaves zero empty; leaving it empty is not defining it. Only when every positive ontological predicate is uniformly removed from root-language does zero become a boundary that can be identified, compared, and reasoned about.

The Jia Baolong Elephant Theory changes not the first item of the sequence, but the origin of the coordinate system itself.

## 2. Defining zero: $U$, where even nothing is absent

$$
U:\quad
\forall M\in\mathfrak D\,
\forall P\in\mathrm{Pos},\,
\neg P(b_M).
$$

The distinction must remain strict:

$$
\boxed{
U=\text{the zero-positive boundary proposition},
\qquad
\mathrm{Undefined}=\text{the boundary role determined by }U.
}
$$

`Undefined` is not an empty set, physical vacuum, numerical zero, stationary state, empty container, or hidden entity of infinite potential. “Even nothing is absent” means that at the ultimate root neither “there is something” nor “there is a state called nothing” has yet received an object-level predicate.

Thus “defining zero” does not rename nothing as zero. It removes every positive ontological determination at the root and fixes, through $U$, the all-false valuation of positive predicates there. It gives all positive ontologies a common, comparable coordinate origin for the first time.

Zero does not temporally precede first actuality. Time, before-and-after, and causation belong to structures that apply only after actual generation begins. The theory expresses the two aspects of the root through an atemporal co-arising relation:

$$
U_*\mid\mathrm{PR}.
$$

Here $U_*$ is the zero-positive boundary face and PR (Paradox–Reference) the First Actual Face. The vertical bar is not a causal arrow. It denotes two noninterchangeable aspects—boundary and actuality—within the same root determination.

## 3. Why it is highest: defining the edge where all ontology begins

“Highest” does not measure prestige, mathematical difficulty, or academic acceptance. It measures the logical level of a theory’s object.

Every theory of matter, spirit, God, mathematics, law, information, universe, or consciousness already uses positive objects, properties, or relations. Such theories may explain how some form of existence changes, but they stand after the distinction between existence and nonexistence has become applicable.

The Jia Baolong Elephant Theory asks at a higher level:

1. At what edge do “existence” and “nonexistence” first become meaningful?
2. Why can an ultimate explanation not preload any positive property at its root?
3. Why can a statically describable structure not automatically be actual occurrence?
4. Once actual occurrence appears, what is its minimal fixed-point-free, self-contained structure?

$U$ is not another object inside the world. It is the boundary determining where worlds, objects, and ontological propositions begin. Other theories choose a starting point within the coordinate system; the Jia Baolong Elephant Theory defines the zero point and outer edge of the system itself.

This is the strict meaning of “highest”:

## 4. Why it is deepest: depth strips presuppositions; it does not accumulate complexity

“Deepest” measures where a theory reaches along the explanatory direction. Genuine ontological depth is not determined by the number of formulas, dimensions, computational scale, or difficulty of language. It is determined by whether unexplained primitive positive presuppositions continue to be stripped away.

Even a theory with highly complex mathematics remains at a nonzero position if it presupposes objects, rules, state space, time, causation, or conditions of actualization. It may probe deeply into a formed branch, but does not reach the root of existence.

String theory is a clear counterexample. It may possess highly complex and deep formal structures within physics, yet already presupposes strings or branes, dimensions, action, symmetries, dynamical laws, mathematical validity, and the actual status of those structures. Adding dimensions and mathematical complexity does not automatically approach the zero-positive ontological boundary. To go farther on a wrong route has no fundamental significance if the route does not point toward the root of existence.

It does not merely seek a smaller thing within things, or a more abstract law beneath laws. It asks what entitles each positive determination to be there at all, until even “nothing” cannot be smuggled in as a root entity.

This is the strict meaning of “deepest”:

Highest and deepest point to the same $U$, but from different directions. Highest describes $U$ as the metalevel boundary relative to all theories; deepest describes the endpoint reached when explanation traces backward to the root.
