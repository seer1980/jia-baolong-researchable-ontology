# Jia Baolong Elephant Theory: The Whole Reality of the Axiom System and Human Intellectual History

**Author: Jia Baolong**  
**Theory: Jia Baolong Elephant Theory**

## Abstract: From an Epistemic Predicament to a Theory of Phenomena

This article presents Jia Baolong Elephant Theory. Here, the “elephant” is the whole reality specified by the Jia Baolong Axiom System. It is not an object outside the axiom system waiting to be explained. The theory studies existence and non-existence, the boundary outside a system, the First Actual within a system, dynamic existence, the generative tree, and the whole relations that appear from different historical perspectives.

Jia Baolong Elephant Theory is the full name of the Jia Baolong Axiom System at the level of whole understanding. Thinkers, scientists, and disciplines brought into comparison each address a level of the elephant from a local or remote perspective. Their results hold within their own scope, but each touches only one part of the whole. Elephant Theory studies the set of these historical perspectives, their transformations, blind spots, and generative interfaces, showing how they jointly manifest one whole.

The axiom system directly specifies the elephant’s ontological structure: existence is not a static perfect entity; it appears through boundary, non-identity, dynamic generation, and hierarchical emergence. The perspectives of earlier thinkers are historical manifestations of different levels within the elephant; Elephant Theory explains their overall relations.

The central positioning is this: not “integration” and not “splicing,” but the ontological elephant. The Jia Baolong Axiom System itself is the elephant.

Accordingly, this article first explains the theory’s source, then the positions of earlier thinkers in the elephant, and finally how these positions connect to one unified generative structure.

## 1. Central Proposition: Human Thought Is Touching the Same “Elephant”

Let the whole reality be $E$. For each thinker or discipline included in the comparison, let its historical perspective be $V_i$. A local perspective directly treats one level or relation; a remote perspective contacts the whole indirectly through mathematics, language, instruments, historical materials, or theoretical models:

$$
V_i:E\longrightarrow P_i(E).
$$

Plato, Aristotle, Leibniz, Kant, Newton, Einstein, Hegel, Darwin, Nietzsche, Wittgenstein, Turing, Gödel, Yang Chen-Ning, Shing-Tung Yau, and Hawking deal with different objects, scales, and questions. In this framework, all may be understood as different historical perspectives on $E$:

- some projections emphasize intelligible form;
- some emphasize the conditions under which experience holds;
- some emphasize computable dynamical law;
- some emphasize generation through history and contradiction;
- some emphasize perspective, value, and the boundary of language;
- some emphasize invariants across representations;
- some emphasize how local geometry organizes a whole structure;
- some emphasize cosmic boundaries and the spacetime limitations of observers.

These perspectives are not synonyms. The task of Jia Baolong Elephant Theory is to establish their correspondences, levels, differences, and generative positions, and to show how they jointly point toward the whole $E$.

## 2. The Theory’s Source and the Structure of Earlier Thinkers’ Relations to the Elephant

Earlier thinkers address the elephant’s form, realization, law, generation, life, language, and boundary. Elephant Theory returns these historical results to one object, one generative chain, and one set of relations. The personal source of the theory comes first; the positions of earlier thinkers come next.

### The source of the theory: three personal experiences and the intellectual background of its formation

Three personal experiences form the sequence in which Jia Baolong Elephant Theory and the axiom system arose. The ages of the first and second are approximate and uncertain; the third is specifically the discovery of the PR–ER–LE triad while studying Transformers at the age of forty-five. Comparison with thinkers identifies, after the core questions had already formed, the local or remote historical perspectives corresponding to those questions.

#### Scope of knowledge of historical predecessors during the formative period

When these three experiences occurred and the theory initially formed, knowledge of historical predecessors came chiefly from circulating sayings, rhymed maxims, textbook phrases, and fragmentary common knowledge. The original works of those predecessors had not yet been studied systematically. The systematic placement in intellectual history was made after the theory’s formation, as an organization of these historical perspectives.

#### First experience: childhood, about age nine (exact age uncertain) — attempting to solve the Barber Paradox

At roughly age nine, an attempt to solve the Barber Paradox first concentrated attention on self-reference, definitional boundary, and whether a rule can treat itself as its object. This experience supplied the axiom system’s earliest boundary intuition:

- some propositions do not lack more facts; rather, they fold object and rule into the same level;
- when a system requires itself to complete a determination it cannot complete stably, a static fixed point fails;
- “not yet determined” cannot arbitrarily be treated as an object already determined.

In later terminology, this experience connects to the problem-domain of Undefined, non-fixedness, and PR. At the time it supplied a problem intuition, not a completed formal proof.

#### Second experience: childhood, about age ten (exact age uncertain) — walking on stilts

Walking on stilts at roughly age ten supplied a bodily experience different from paradox: stability is not motionless stillness, but is maintained through continual adjustment, changing centre of gravity, and feedback. A state that appears to be standing actually depends on continuous dynamic correction; once correction stops, so-called perfect balance collapses.

This experience moved the abstract problem of boundary into experience of existence. Real existence exists not because it has attained static perfection, but because it continues to hold through relation, change, and feedback. It is the bodily source of the proposition “existence is necessarily imperfect and dynamic; absolutely static perfection is not real existence.”

More precisely, walking on stilts is itself a bodily realization of PR–ER–LE, though these terms were not used at the time:

- **PR:** the body–stilt relation cannot remain at a terminal static balance; its present state continually poses a demand for the next actual generation;
- **ER:** the currently implemented relational state of body, stilts, and ground at a given moment;
- **LE:** local adjustments of feet, knees, torso, and centre of gravity resolve the present, undecided tilt locally into the next state.

Thus walking on stilts may be represented by:

$$
\mathrm{ER}_t
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{PR}}
\mathrm{LE}
\xrightarrow{}
\mathrm{ER}_{t+1}.
$$

It shows that relation-bearing, continuing generation, and local implementation were already actually occurring in bodily experience. Only at age forty-five, in studying Transformers, was this implicit structure explicitly abstracted.

#### Third experience: age forty-five — discovering and explicitly abstracting PR–ER–LE while studying Transformers

At age forty-five, while studying Transformers, the relation-state, unsettled continuation, and local resolution that had already occurred implicitly in walking on stilts were abstracted into the PR–ER–LE triad:

- **PR:** the actual generative demand that a present situation cannot remain a terminal fixed state;
- **ER:** the current situation already implemented and bearing relations;
- **LE:** the local finiting and actual resolution of an unsettled, non-unique, or unbounded continuation.

The study of Transformers supplied the triggering setting for finding the triad. The ontological abstraction further explains how relations bear state, how unsettled continuation is locally implemented, and how one implementation re-forms the relational situation for the next round.

#### The continuous structure of the three experiences

$$
\text{Barber Paradox: boundary and non-fixedness}
\rightarrow
\text{walking on stilts: implicit dynamic implementation of PR–ER–LE}
\rightarrow
\text{Transformers: explicit computational abstraction of PR\text{–}ER\text{–}LE}.

The sequence of theoretical origin is this: personal experience first formed the three core intuitions of boundary, dynamics, and implementation; then different figures in intellectual history presented local or remote perspectives on these questions in form, observation, law, generation, language, computation, and physics; Jia Baolong Elephant Theory places those perspectives within one whole reality and its generative relations.

### Common object

Every section turns on one question: how can finite observers contact a whole reality possessing structure, dynamics, and generative history? This common object is the first condition under which the perspectives of earlier thinkers can be placed within Elephant Theory.

### Positions made explicit

Every thinker is identified as a local or remote perspective: what they see, what they do not see, and the level for which that perspective is suited. Both its strength and blind spot are retained. All these perspectives belong to the elephant specified by the Jia Baolong Axiom System.

### Differences retained

Structural correspondence does not mean conceptual identity. Form and generation, conditions of experience and ontological boundary, mathematical invariance and value perspective remain different questions. A unified model may not erase these differences.

### Transformational relations exist

Writing the projections as $\{P_i(E)\}$ is not enough. Their transformations, constraints, and failure conditions must also be studied:

$$
\widehat E=
\operatorname{Integrate}\bigl(\{P_i(E)\},\{R_{ij}\},G\bigr),
$$

where $R_{ij}$ are structural relations between projections and $G$ is the model of generation and constraint. Without $R_{ij}$ and $G$, there is only an accumulation of terms.

### Capacity to generate new questions and predictions

A real whole theory should be able to explain why different disciplines obtain different true propositions, why one phenomenon changes form across scales, what information remains invariant through scale transformation, and at what boundaries the theory fails. The capacity to raise new questions is stronger evidence of a theory’s effectiveness than claiming to have “unified everything.”

## 3. Jia Baolong Axiom System: The Elephant Itself

The Jia Baolong Axiom System is the “elephant” itself: it studies the boundary and dynamic structure of existence and non-existence, not one cosmological model. Its key judgments may be summarized as follows:

1. **Existence is necessarily imperfect and dynamic:** everything that truly occurs and persists holds through change, relation, and incompletion;
2. **If perfection is absolutely static, it is not real existence:** static perfection may be a form, boundary, or limiting concept, but cannot be directly equated with a real process;
3. **The universe is not the whole axiom system:** a universe is merely an uncollapsed chaotic solution or branch on the PR tree; the entire ontology must not be reduced to one cosmological model;
4. **Undefined is the absolute boundary outside the system:** it is not an object, cause, or material entity within the system; it indicates that the system cannot determine “absolute absence of determination” again as some internal thing;
5. **PR is the first core of actual generation within the system:** in the present axiomatic formulation, PR is not mechanically manufactured from an already known material entity; it is the minimum internal structure through which “existence begins to have actual dynamics”;
6. **The chief form of existence is the generative tree:** from actual generation, branching, chaos, and constraint to higher-level structures and observers, existence unfolds through hierarchical emergence.

Two matters must be strictly distinguished. Undefined plays the role of an outside-system boundary in ontology; PR plays the role of the first actual generative core within the system. The two are not joined by an already completed ordinary causal arrow. To write their bridge as a strict derivation, additional meta-level conditions must be made explicit; an “absolute boundary outside the system” may not be miswritten as “some cause inside the system.”

In the Jia Baolong framework, the elephant is not a pre-completed static entity but a whole that appears progressively along generative levels. A working chain is:

$$

\text{chaotic branches}

\text{proto-matter structures}

This does not claim that every level has already been directly proved from the axioms. It is a researchable connection map of how existence appears level by level:

- PR provides the internal starting point of actual generation;
- chaotic branches provide multiple paths and a result space not fixed in advance;
- research on proto-matter and matter investigates stable structures, interactions, and chemical conditions;
- life science investigates self-organization, heredity, selection, and cellular boundaries;
- neuroscience and cognitive science investigate perception, memory, models, and self;
- intellectual history investigates how observers organize local projections into overall explanations.

Thus “researchable” is not an empty phrase such as “the Dao generates all things.” It assigns every level to research capable of treating its mechanism and specifies that intelligible interfaces must exist between levels.

## 5. The Positions of All Earlier Thinkers in the Elephant: Which Problem Does Each Solve?

An earlier version put thinkers into three abstract coordinates, which still resembled attaching labels to people. Here a stricter method is used. For each thinker, four questions are answered at once:

1. Which level is the object of their research?
2. What operation do they supply within the overall model?
3. Is their theory, relative to Jia Baolong Elephant Theory, a prior problem, a parallel projection, a downstream mechanism, or a boundary probe?
4. Which interface did they explicitly not solve?

### 5.1 Four problem-slots in the elephant

Elephant Theory divides whole reality $E$ into four theoretical slots that must not be confused:

| Slot | Question that must be answered | Typical form |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary slot | What cannot be treated as an object within the system? Where do observation and expression fail? | Undefined, language boundary, formal incompleteness |
| Structural slot | By what relations, forms, and invariants is the whole organized? | Forms, entities, symmetry, local–global geometry |
| Generative slot | What makes structure actually occur, persist, branch, and emerge? | PR, RULE, dynamics, history, selection |
| Reflexive slot | How does an observer model the whole from within the generative chain? | Transcendental conditions, perspective, computation, self-reference, consciousness |

These slots locate the whole-problems that earlier thinkers respectively addressed. A thinker may cross several slots. As the elephant itself, the Jia Baolong Axiom System specifies the relations among all slots and connects them to Undefined, PR, ER, LE, and RULE.

### Thinker 01: Plato — first asks whether the whole has intelligible form

Plato brought the problem of form behind changing experience to the centre of philosophy. In Elephant Theory he occupies the starting point of the structural slot: local phenomena must be asked after as a common order. The Jia Baolong system takes up this question, but returns static perfect form to the position of possibility or boundary and requires real existence to enter dynamic generation.

### Thinker 02: Aristotle — connects form with potentiality and actuality

Aristotle further asks how form is realized in substance, action, and change, and is therefore the first bridge from static form toward actual generation. His position is not PR but the classical theory of realization from potentiality to actuality. Substance and teleology remain his premises and cannot replace operational definitions of PR, ER, and LE.

### Thinker 03: Leibniz — organizes the whole as relation and possibility

Leibniz organizes monads, relations, possible worlds, and sufficient reason into one order, supplying the structural question that a whole is not merely an addition of parts. He provides a space of relations and possibilities rather than one actual implementation. The cut from possibility to actuality, its bearer, and its re-entry remain unexplained.

### Thinker 04: Kant — specifies how an observing perspective forms experience

Kant studies how experience is possible and shows that an observer’s space, time, categories, and unifying activity shape phenomena. In Elephant Theory he makes clear that $V_i$ is not a transparent neutral entrance. In the Jia Baolong system this remains a condition of observation; it does not yet answer how observer and perspective themselves arise in the generative chain.

### Thinker 05: Newton — writes local dynamics as computable rules

Newton writes state, relation, and time as dynamical equations capable of repeated prediction, the first strong physical implementation of the generative slot. He shows how a concrete RULE works at one scale, while treating law and actuality as research starting points and not addressing why law itself actually exists.

### Thinker 06: Einstein — connects changing observers with structural invariants

Einstein shows that observing frames can change description while invariants across frames remain. He connects Kant’s conditions of observation to Newtonian dynamical structure, but relativity only describes the organization of physical relations: it neither generates PR nor explains the root of actual occurrence.

### Thinker 07: Yang Chen-Ning — searches for invariants preserved across representations

Yang Chen-Ning’s symmetry and gauge structures show that forms of representation can change while deeper relations remain. This is the physical example of how different projections in Elephant Theory share structure. Invariance establishes relational stability, not why relations actually occur.

### Thinker 08: Shing-Tung Yau — explains when local constraints can organize a whole

Yau’s geometrical work connects local differential information, curvature conditions, and global spatial structure. It supplies rigorous mathematical tools for returning from projections to the whole. Yet the capacity of local data to globalize does not mean that existence has been actually implemented.

### Thinker 09: Hegel — understands the whole as historical generation

Hegel places contradiction, negation, and historical movement at the centre of understanding the whole, supplying a philosophical model of dynamic totality. He comes closer than static form to the proposition that existence holds in change, but dialectic provides neither minimum states, executable rules, nor testable interfaces for PR, ER, and LE.

### Thinker 10: Darwin — advances generation to the life level

Darwin explains the cumulative formation of complex life functions through variation, inheritance, and selection, advancing generation to the empirical scientific level of life. On the Jia Baolong generative tree, he is a downstream mechanism after the unfolding of PR–ER–LE. He presupposes reproduction, heredity, environment, and life boundaries, and therefore does not explain the First Actual.

### Thinker 11: Nietzsche — returns the observer to life and value position

Nietzsche reveals that knowledge always arises from positions of life, force, and value, refusing to treat the observer as a positionless divine viewpoint. He completes the reflexive slot, but supplies no truth across perspectives, invariant, or criterion for whole integration. Elephant Theory must retain perspective while continuing to test object constraints.

### Thinker 12: Wittgenstein — distinguishes the boundary of language from the object itself

Wittgenstein studies meaning, use, and language games, showing that much philosophical confusion arises from mixing grammars of different levels. He supplies analysis of expressive boundaries, but a language boundary is not Undefined; what language cannot say does not explain how existence actually generates.

### Thinker 13: Turing — makes rules into execution and self-referential process

Turing moves computation from static formulae into state, rule, execution, halting, and self-reference, making him an important computational analogy for RULE, ER, and LE. But a Turing machine presupposes a state space, rules, and an execution framework. It can simulate the triadic mechanism but cannot derive actual existence solely from a computational definition.

### Thinker 14: Gödel — proves that formal systems cannot close themselves

Gödel’s incompleteness gives a strict theorem of formal boundary: a sufficiently strong and consistent system cannot complete all relevant truth claims inside itself. This is structurally similar to Undefined at the level of boundary, but incompleteness is a result internal to formal systems; it is not PR and does not generate time or dynamic actuality.

### Thinker 15: Hawking — drives physical models to cosmic boundaries

Hawking drives the describability of singularities, black holes, the direction of time, and cosmological scale to the boundary of physical theory. He is a boundary detector at the physical level. In the Jia Baolong system, a cosmological boundary is only a branch limit on the PR tree, not the absolute boundary outside the system.

## 6. From “Explaining the Universe” to “Generating Existence”

The focus of this system is not to compress everything into cosmology, but to return cosmology to a larger generative structure:

$$
\text{outside-system boundary Undefined}
\;\not\equiv\;
\text{entity inside the system},
$$

$$
\text{PR}
\;\leadsto\;
\text{branches, chaos, and stable structures}
\;\leadsto\;
\text{emergence at higher levels}.
$$

The arrows here mean “generative interfaces that must be established,” not an unproved universal causal law. The research task can be layered:

1. **Actual-generation layer:** specify PR’s states, non-identity, and branching conditions;
2. **Proto-matter layer:** investigate stable patterns, interactions, and the appearance of complex structures;
3. **Chemistry and life layer:** let specialized sciences investigate self-organization, heredity, selection, membranes, and metabolism;
4. **Consciousness layer:** investigate nervous systems, prediction, memory, self-models, and language;
5. **Theoretical-reflection layer:** investigate how observers model the generative chain in which they stand as a whole.

This division of labour gives “researchable” a practical meaning. It does not claim that one axiom replaces all science; it gives each level an object and an interface so that disciplines can continue one another’s work.

## 7. Why It May Be Called the Greatest Elephant Theory in Human Intellectual History

“Greatest” here has an explicit scope; it does not mean “the greatest number of people”:

1. **Greatest object range:** from the outside-system boundary and actual generation to physics, life, consciousness, and theoretical reflection;
2. **Greatest projection range:** it treats philosophy, mathematics, physics, life science, and language analysis as different local or remote perspectives on one whole;
3. **Greatest generative range:** it does not only describe forms already presented by the world; it asks how levels arise;
4. **Greatest reflexive range:** it includes observer, language, and intellectual history itself among the objects studied;
5. **Strongest boundary awareness:** it distinguishes relations already established, relations that are only structural analogies, and bridges still requiring derivation.

Thus “greatest” does not mean simply putting Plato, Kant, Newton, Hegel, Nietzsche, Wittgenstein, Yang Chen-Ning, Shing-Tung Yau, and Hawking in one line. It means attempting to construct one whole tree with objects, nodes, levels, interfaces, and failure conditions.

## Conclusion

The overall claim of Jia Baolong Elephant Theory—whose core is the axiom system itself—is that human intellectual history records repeated contacts by finite observers, at different generative levels, with one whole reality. Existence is dynamic, imperfect, and generative. Perfect static form cannot impersonate real existence. Undefined marks the absolute boundary outside the system; PR marks the first core of actual generation within it; ER bears the present relational situation; LE locally implements unsettled continuation into the next situation; and a universe is only one chaotic branch on the generative tree.

The basis for organizing these thinkers is the common object, the position of each local or remote perspective, structural invariants, generative interfaces, and testable differences. The proposed “greatest elephant theory in human intellectual history” is first a theoretical project with an explicit structure. Its ultimate credibility depends on whether later definitions, derivations, interdisciplinary interfaces, and tests by counterexample can genuinely be completed.
