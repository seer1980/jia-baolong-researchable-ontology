Focus II: Jia Baolong Absolute Truth

Jia Baolong

Name and Strict Object

Jia Baolong Absolute Truth (JBAT) does not mean that the word Undefined is itself a mysterious object or an unconditionally true proposition. Its strict object is the boundary proposition:

\[ U:\quad \forall M\in\mathfrak D\, \forall P\in\mathrm{Pos},\, \neg P(b_M). \]

This means that, in the specified admissible class of models \(\mathfrak D\), every terminal root position \(b_M\) carries no positive ontological determination \(P\).

Undefined is the name of the terminal boundary role that satisfies this zero-positive condition.

Research Task

\(\mathfrak D\) is not a set of arbitrary worldviews. It is the class of explanatory models claiming to complete one and the same task:

  • explain actual occurrence rather than merely give a describable structure;
  • admit no pre-existing positive actuality at the root;
  • borrow neither an external executor nor an external substrate;
  • terminate explanatory regress;
  • avoid letting positive contents mutually guarantee one another in a circle;
  • avoid declaring positive content arbitrarily exempt from explanation;
  • avoid equating static encoding with actual occurrence;
  • account for the identity, distinction, rule, domain, or actuality condition required by positive root content.

A philosophical system that declines this task is not thereby proven grammatically contradictory; it simply has not completed the selected explanatory task of “from nothing to something.”

Proof Skeleton

Explanatory Closure

Any positive root content requires at least one condition that makes it intelligible. Following the graph of explanatory dependence, one can only:

  1. arrive at an external endpoint;
  2. arrive at an arbitrary endpoint;
  3. substitute a static description for actuality;
  4. arrive at a zero-positive boundary;
  5. repeat a positive circle;
  6. regress infinitely;
  7. end at a terminal root that is already a zero-positive boundary.

The first six possibilities cannot simultaneously satisfy the specified task of completeness, non-arbitrariness, and self-containment. A root that completes the task therefore cannot retain positive ontological content.

Boundary-Truth Theorem

\[ \forall M\in\mathfrak D,\ \forall P\in\mathrm{Pos},\ \neg P(b_M). \]

This proposition remains invariant across admissible models and does not depend on the internal physical laws, entities, or histories of any individual model.

Fundamental Uniqueness

The specified root language consists only of positive root predicates and their logical combinations. At every terminal root, all such positive atoms are false. \(U\) fixes the complete all-false boundary valuation and therefore entails every other root invariant in that language. If any other fundamental generative proposition also entails all root invariants, it must mutually entail \(U\):

\[ G\leftrightarrow U. \]

Uniqueness is calculated by logical equivalence class.

The Five Precise Meanings of “Absolute”

  1. Model invariance: it holds in every model in \(\mathfrak D\).
  2. Content independence: changes in positive internal content do not alter \(U\).
  3. Non-empirical status: it does not depend on observing any particular universe.
  4. Maximal root scope: the selected task permits no deeper positive root.
  5. Cross-framework transferability: theories that use different terms while completing the same task inherit the zero-positive-boundary conclusion.

Here, absoluteness means the absence of positive ontological presuppositions at the root; logical expression still uses language, identity, and norms of inference.

The Role of \(99.999\ldots\%=100\%\)

\[ 99.999\ldots\%=100\% \]

reveals only a form of cognition: on the surface, the final small segment seems forever missing, yet the limiting whole is already strictly complete. It helps one understand how a boundary proposition that is not positively closed can still be complete, but it does not prove \(U\). The actual proof comes from explanatory closure, elimination of positive content, cross-model boundary equivalence, and fundamental uniqueness.

Relation to the First Beat

JBAT gives the root’s zero-positive boundary. In an actualized model, the First Beat appears as:

\[ U\mid\mathrm{PR}. \]

Actuality, self-containment, the backward binary quotient, persistent correction, and non-fixed conditions jointly yield PR as the normal form of the First Beat.
